This page says "No town is in code yet" and gives Cogfall five invented buildings. Both are wrong, and they came from docs/grove-towns-design.md ("Nothing is in code yet"), which is stale. TOWNS holds seven live towns, acts.js holds Acts I–VII with quests and tool-slot perks, TownClient renders a parallax street, and 57 town sprites are on disk. The real shape of a town is three tiling plates plus a centrepiece, a vault, a questgiver and one building per feature — so Cogfall is seven files at $0.36, not a design question. Corrected page: docs/town-8-cogfall-art.html. Everything below about the statue tier, the seventeen craftables and the arrival moment still stands.

Cogfall

Built into a stopped gear the size of a church. The teeth are the stairs.

Towns 1–7 each exist to open a feature, and the towns doc is blunt about what that is worth: the unlock moment “should be the loudest thing in the game.” Town 8 has nothing left to open. A town that arrives with a store and a shrug is the anticlimax at the end of twenty hours of climbing.

Cogfall already has its loudest moment, and it is already in the code. That is the finding this page is built around, and nothing below asks for a new feature.

What is already there

World 8 is the statue vendor's second tier

Shipped last night, in grove-statues.js

VENDOR_TIER_MAP = { 1: 2, 2: 8, 3: 12 }

Your own words, in the comment above it: “the vendor base form is part of world 2. And statues dont start dropping until world 2… maybe at world 8 and 12 unlocks new tiers of statue vendors allowing you to find different rarity of statues.” Reaching world 8 is tier 2 — the tier where crystal statues start to drop.

So the statue vendor is not a building Cogfall happens to contain. It is what Cogfall is for, and the arrival moment writes itself: the player who has walked seven maps is told that the kill count behind them is about to be worth considerably more.

Every statue that falls after Cogfall is worth 45% more than every statue that fell before it — and all three rarities pour into the same bar, so a crystal is simply four regulars rather than a thing you have to collect separately.

And the first crystal of each line doubles it, permanently

Not every crystal — the first. Five lines, five doublings, each one a thing you watched happen, all five of them waiting on the other side of this town. There is deliberately no “turn in all”, so none of it can be spent inside a loop.

⚠️ Towns 9, 10 and 11 have nothing like this

Tier 3 is town 12, so the two ends are covered and the middle three are not. I am not going to invent three moments to fill the gap — that is how the feature proposals got cut in the first place. It is worth knowing that Cogfall is the easy one and Hoarfast is the hard one.

The second moment, also free

Twelve ladders gain a rung at once

A recipe's reachability is already keyed to its map — reachableRecipes filters on mapIsBuilt(recipeMap(r)). So the instant map 8 opens, tier VIII of all twelve tool ladders becomes makeable in the same breath, out of materials the player is about to be holding too many of.

That is seventeen objects at once: twelve tools, four foods and the charm. No code, no gate, no new screen — the bench simply has seventeen rows it did not have an hour ago. Cogfall is the first town in the game whose reason to exist is the workbench rather than a door, and that is a fair description of every town after it too.

The seventeen are specified in full in the maps 8–12 draft — The Ratchet Rod, The Shearhead, The Drop Forge, the Mainspring Draught, The Stopped Hour and the rest.

The place

A town inside one tooth of something enormous

A gear the size of a church stopped turning a long time ago, about a third of the way through a rotation, and it stopped at an angle. Cogfall is built in the wedge that left: the rim is the street, the teeth are the stairs down to it, and every floor is slightly out of level in the same direction, so nothing in town stands quite straight and everybody has stopped noticing.

It is the first manufactured town in the game. Towns 1–7 are built out of wood, stone, rope and ash — things found on the ground where the town sits. Cogfall is built out of brass, chain, felt and lead, and not one piece of it was made here. That difference is the whole art direction and it costs nothing extra to honour: the same buildings, in a vocabulary the game has never used for a place people live.

The machine is not dead, which is the one thing the backdrop has to say. Something in the middle distance is still turning. Nobody in Cogfall will tell you what.

⚠️ Nothing is shuttered, and that is a visual change nobody has specified

Towns 1–7 are designed around locked buildings: “six buildings, four of them visibly shuttered with a line saying which map opens them”, and the rule is locked, not hidden — a dimmed sprite that says which map opens it. That dimmed row is the only advertisement the late game gets.

Cogfall has nothing left to shutter. Every door in town 8 is lit, and so is every door in 9 through 12. A player who has learned to read a street by its dark windows arrives somewhere with none — which can read as finished or as empty, and the difference is worth one deliberate decision rather than falling out of the implementation. My call: the late towns should look lit and small, five bright windows rather than six with four out, so the contrast says arrival rather than absence.

Foreman Gall · Coliseum, zone 8

“The floor turns whether you're on it or not. Two minutes.”

The longest clock of any Coliseum, because nothing on this map dies fast. Waves are small and armoured and the wave count is the score — the ring that rewards raw damage.

Verb mix: cull 3 · delve 2 · harvest 2 · survive 1 · forge 1 · ticket 1 · hunt 1. Cull-heavy, because on the slowest-killing map in the game a kill quota is the honest ask.

The Tallyman is the resident who should be told about statues. He is already a man who counts bodies for a living, standing in the town where body count starts paying 45% more — his third quest, A Number Worth Having, is a Coliseum ticket today and could just as easily be the line that explains the vendor.

The gap

The statue vendor has no face

Cogfall is the statue town, so this is the part that matters most. Right now the statue system ships with no art at all:

  • The menu row that opens it borrows the emblems icon — art: "/images/grove/ui-emblems.webp". A real door, pointed at somebody else's sprite.
  • A statue is a coloured chip. style={{ "--r": r.colour }} — the rarity is a hex value on a CSS variable, which is the closest thing to a glyph the Grove has shipped.
  • There is no vendor. The thing you turn statues in to is a panel heading.

Five statues, three rarities each — a closed fist, open lungs, a faceless figure with a very good shield, a half-carved swing, and something kneeling and getting up. Fifteen objects whose whole job is to feel worth hoarding, currently rendered as swatches.

And this is not a map 8 cost

Statues drop from world 2. Every member deep enough to see them is looking at the chips today, so the fifteen sprites and the vendor pay off across the whole game and merely land in Cogfall. It is the cheapest high-visibility art left in the Grove.

What needs your call

Four things, in the order they block

  1. Is the statue tier the arrival moment? I think it plainly is, and it needs no code — but it does mean the town's whole announcement is a stat system rather than a new room, and you should want that before I build the beat around it.
  2. The fifteen statues and the vendor. $0.87 of the bill and the only part that changes something members can see today. I would draw these first, before any of map 8.
  3. Lit and small, or six with one out? The late towns have nothing to shutter. My call is five lit windows and no dark ones, so the street reads as arrival — but the locked-building language is load-bearing in towns 1–7 and dropping it at town 8 is a real change of grammar.
  4. Towns 9, 10 and 11 still have no moment. Tier 3 is town 12. I am deliberately not inventing three replacements; if the answer is that the middle three are simply quieter towns, that is a fine answer and I will write them that way.
