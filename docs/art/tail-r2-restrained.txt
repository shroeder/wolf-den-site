 THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RULE, CHECK IT BEFORE FINISHING: the image must contain the character and ABSOLUTELY NOTHING ELSE on a fully transparent background. Do NOT draw a shadow. Do NOT draw a shadow ellipse, oval, pool, puddle, disc or grey smudge on the ground under the feet. There is no ground and no floor — the character is floating against empty transparency. A drop shadow beneath the boots is the most common mistake made on this task and it RUINS the sprite, because the art is cut out and placed on other backgrounds where a shadow appears as a grey stain. The pixels around and below the character must be fully transparent and completely empty. HOUSE STYLE — THESE RULES OVERRIDE ANY CONTRARY INSTINCT ABOUT WHAT A HEROIC CHARACTER SHOULD LOOK LIKE. PROPORTIONS: draw the hero as a STOCKY CHIBI — a large head about one third of the total body height, a short sturdy torso, short legs, big hands and big boots, standing squarely on both feet with a wide planted stance. This is deliberately NOT a realistically proportioned figure and NOT a tall slender heroic one; a tall figure is wrong even if it looks more impressive. RENDERING: flat cel shading with only two or three tone steps per colour, large simple areas of solid colour, and a bold dark outline around the whole figure and around every major shape inside it. No airbrushed or soft gradients, no painterly rendering, no photographic or cinematic lighting, no rim light tracing every edge, no fine filigree and no hundreds of tiny marks — a few large shapes must read better than many small ones. BACKGROUND: fully transparent, and draw NOTHING that is not the character itself — no ground, no floor, no cast shadow, no contact shadow, no ellipse or smudge beneath the feet, no vignette, no background glow. EFFECTS: the magical aura is a small number of discrete floating wisps and sparks near the gear, never a full-body glow, and never an elemental effect that was not named — do not add fire, flames, smoke, ice, or lightning unless an item is named for it. WEAPONS: draw each named weapon as the kind of weapon its name states — a maul is a heavy blunt hammer and must never be drawn as an axe or a blade. FACE: keep the reference's facial hair exactly as shown — if the reference has a beard, the hero has that same beard, and the hair colour matches the reference rather than being changed.
 THERE IS A SECOND IMAGE ATTACHED AND IT IS A STYLE GUIDE, NOT A SUBJECT. It is a sheet of finished assets from this game, drawn in the exact house style you must match. Copy from it the LINE WEIGHT (a thick dark outline around every form), the FLATNESS (large areas of solid colour, two or three tone steps, no painterly blending or airbrushed gradients), and the SIMPLICITY (a few large shapes per form, almost no small ornament). Do NOT copy any object, character, colour scheme or item from that sheet — only how it is DRAWN. The first image is the subject whose identity you keep. COUNTABLE LIMITS, AND THEY ARE HARD LIMITS. Build each arm, each leg and the torso from TWO OR THREE large shapes, not from a stack of overlapping plates. Put AT MOST THREE gems on the entire figure. Gold appears only as a THIN EDGE along the border of a shape — never as a filled panel, never as a second layer of trim on top of trim. THINGS THAT MUST NOT APPEAR UNLESS AN ITEM IS NAMED FOR THEM: fur collars, feathered mantles, shoulder ruffs, animal heads, dragon emblems, crests, heraldry, engraved patterns, scrollwork, filigree, studs, rivets, chains, tassels, or any emblem printed on the cape. THE CAPE IS ONE FLAT COLOUR with a plain edge — no motif, no pattern, no gradient, no glow. If you are deciding whether to add a detail, DO NOT ADD IT. The finished sprite must read clearly at 100 pixels tall, and every extra ornament makes it read worse.
