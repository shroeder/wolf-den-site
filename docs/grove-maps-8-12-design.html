THE WOLF DEN · GROVE · DESIGN SPEC · DRAFT FOR REVIEW

Maps 8 to 12

Five maps, 60 zones, 60 bosses, 60 creatures, 60 parts, 20 NPCs, 15 quest chains and 5 Coliseums. Nothing here is built yet. This is the thing to argue with before any of it is.

The brief, and the real risk. Luke: “every time I have you do the work, it tends to all kind of be the same and not very unique… it can't just be, like, vanilla, repeat, repeat.”

That is the correct worry, because the generator makes it easy: a map is twelve parts, twelve creatures, twelve zones and an emblem, and five maps of that shape will come out as one map wearing five coats of paint.

Draft 1 solved it the wrong way and this draft throws that out. Every map had a “signature mechanic” and all five were level-design gimmicks — gear platforms on a cycle, ice that cracked if you stood still, a pulse that doubled damage on the beat, fall-death gaps, a floor that was not continuous. Luke: “I dont want any nifty tropes in the platforming levels. Its not fun for players. They just want to kill stuff.” He is right, and it is worse than a matter of taste: progression in the Grove is kill count, so anything that taxes the kill rate directly lengthens every node — the thing we have spent weeks shortening.

So a map's identity now comes from the roster's behaviour, its density and what it drops. Five ways of killing things, not five floors. Each map also gets a different mix of creature archetypes, a different mix of quest verbs, and its own rare spawn — today there is exactly one rare spawn in the entire game and it is shared by all seven maps.

Where a thing genuinely should repeat, it repeats, and I have said so rather than inventing difference for its own sake.

The five

  • 08The Clockwork Deep — something built thisclockwork
  • 09The Frostmere — black water under white iceice
  • 10The Hollow Colossus — inside a dead titanflesh
  • 11The Cloudshelf — stone that forgot to fallcloud
  • 12The Starfall Rim — past the last airstar

Two names survive from the existing table — The Frostmere and The Starfall Rim, which were already right. Thunder Steppe and Rotgarden are retired: a steppe is grassland and a rotgarden is a swamp, and maps 1, 3 and 4 have covered green and wet thoroughly.

What this had to work around

The feature ladder is spent. Towns 1–7 introduce every system the game has — pets, mining, blacksmith, farm, cooking, sailing, fishing, bounties, dungeons, jeweller, sets, creations, cards, market, auction, trade, casino, spin, arena, boss, leaderboard, trophies, track, badges. There is nothing left for town 8 to unlock. Towns 8–12 therefore deepen instead of introduce, which is a different job and needs your call — proposals are in each chapter and collected at the end.

The map generator is honest about its shape. Each map is exactly 12 parts across 8 roles (edge haft bind hide ember glow stone charm), 12 creatures across 6 archetypes (swarm striker brute caster stalker grazer), 12 zones each with one boss, and one Mark. Food, charm and tool recipes are derived from the parts — so the parts list is the real design surface, and a lazy parts list produces lazy crafting automatically.

Every stat needs a consumer. Two of the five Marks below grant something new, and each names the code that would have to read it. The other three reuse stats already consumed — deliberately, because five new systems in five maps is how a feature becomes a maintenance bill.

08

The Clockwork Deep

“Something built this, and it was never told to stop.”

Signature roster — nothing here flinches Everything is armoured: high health, low damage, and no stagger. You cannot interrupt anything on this map, so a fight is sustained output and target priority rather than reacting. It is the slowest kill rate in the game and it is paid for in salvage — this is where the deeper parts come from. No floor tricks; the platforms are platforms.

The Mark

The Geared Mark — salvage, +1.6%/star.
NEW STAT. A kill has a chance to also drop a part from one map deeper. Consumer: the rare row in grove-roll.js, which already does exactly this for creatureRareLoot — this raises its rate rather than adding a mechanism.

Rare spawn

The Governor — a regulator sphere that spins faster as it loses health, so its telegraphs compress instead of its damage rising. The first rare that gets harder to read rather than harder to survive.

09

The Frostmere

“Black water under white ice, and the ice is the floor.”

Signature roster — it hits you and leaves Ambushers. Things come up through a breathing hole or out of a drift, land one heavy hit, and disengage to reposition rather than standing and trading. Low health, high burst, and the most re-engagements per kill of any map. Frantic without a single thing on the floor moving — the opposite of the Clockwork Deep's immovable wall of armour, and the reason it sits right after it.

The Mark

The Hoar Mark — armour, +3/star.
EXISTING STAT, kept from the current table. A place trying to kill you slowly should give armour, and it is the one combat stat no deep-map Mark currently grants.

Rare spawn

The Mere-Lamp — a light under the ice that moves away as you approach and has to be cornered against a shelf edge. The first rare you catch by geometry rather than damage.

10

The Hollow Colossus

“It died standing, and the inside never got the message.”

Signature roster — one kill becomes three Corpses rupture. Most creatures here split into two or three smaller ones on death, and those have to be cleared too. It is by a wide margin the densest map in the game and the best multi-kill map in it — which is exactly why its Mark is lifesteal, because you will be standing in the middle of the pile. Kill count per minute is the highest of the five until map 11.

The Mark

The Hollow Mark — life_steal, +2.2%/star.
EXISTING STAT, inherited from the retired Rotgarden. Taking life back inside a thing that is still trying to live is the single most on-theme stat in the table, and lifesteal is currently only granted by one map-one wanderer card.

Rare spawn

The Second Heart — heals every creature near it and never attacks. The first rare that is a priority target rather than a fight: ignore it and the room becomes unwinnable.

11

The Cloudshelf

“Stone that forgot to fall, and a long way down.”

Signature roster — they never stop coming Flyers, arriving from off-screen in a continuous stream rather than standing around the zone waiting to be found. No walking to the next fight: the next fight arrives. Highest kills-per-minute in the game, which makes this the farm map — and the Mark makes the stream occasionally produce something worth stopping for.

The Mark

The Shelf Mark — rare_spawn, +1.2%/star.
EXISTING STAT. Draft 1 proposed updraft — jump height. Cut: it is a traversal stat, and traversal is exactly what should not be carrying a map's identity. rare_spawn is currently granted by one card in the whole game, and on the map with the highest throughput it is the stat that turns volume into something worth looking at.

Rare spawn

The Sky-Herd Bull — arrives with a wave rather than spawning in the zone, so it has to be picked out of a crowd and killed before the crowd is. The first rare that is a target priority problem.

The Starfall Rim

“Past the last air. Nothing here is holding anything up.”

Signature roster — few, enormous, and worth it A handful of huge things per zone instead of a crowd. Each takes real commitment and pays many kill credits, so the counter moves in jumps rather than a stream. The exact inverse of the Cloudshelf before it, and the right last note: after a map where you never stop swinging, a map where every engagement is a decision.

The Mark

The Rim Mark — emblem_find, +2.5%/star.
EXISTING STAT, kept from the current table. The right capstone: the last map you reach is the one that makes every other map's collection faster, so finishing the game turns into a reason to go back to the beginning.

What needs your call

  1. Towns 8–12. The feature ladder is spent at town 7. My proposals — foundry, hearth, infirmary, aerie, observatory — all reach back and make an older system matter again rather than add a new one. Four of the five are new code. The cheap alternative is that towns 8–12 are pure waypoints with a store and nothing else, which is honest but dull.
  2. One new stat, down from two. salvage (map 8) is low-risk — it scales a rate that already exists in creatureRareLoot. updraft is cut entirely: it was a traversal stat, and traversal is the thing that should not be carrying a map.
  3. Rupturing corpses (map 10) is the only roster behaviour here that needs new engine work — a kill that spawns smaller foes at the death position. Everything else (no stagger, disengage-after-hit, stream spawning, few-and-huge) is tuning numbers the spawner already has. It is also the one with a real failure mode: a rupture chain that never terminates. Needs a hard depth cap of one split.
  4. Per-map rare spawns. There is one rare spawn in the entire game today, shared by all seven maps. Five new ones is five creatures of art and a GROVE_RARE that becomes a per-map lookup. I think it is the single highest-value item here, because hunt quests currently all point at the same stag.
  5. The infirmary (map 10) is the one proposal that changes the shape of a run rather than adding to it. It deserves its own conversation.
