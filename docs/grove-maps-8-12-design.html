THE WOLF DEN · GROVE · DESIGN SPEC · DRAFT 3 FOR REVIEW

Maps 8 to 12

Five maps, 60 zones, 60 bosses, 60 creatures, 60 parts, 85 authored craftables, 20 NPCs, 15 quest chains and 5 Coliseums. No new mechanics, no new systems, and nothing asked of the seven maps that already work.

The brief, corrected. Luke: “the uniqueness I was talking about and the lack of variety was more about crafting, not crazy weird tropes and making things all different… I don't want to mess with the base game.” And: “I was hoping that, like, crafting and items would be unique.”

Two drafts got this wrong in two different directions. Draft 1 gave every map a level-design gimmick — gear platforms on a cycle, ice that cracked if you stood still, fall-death gaps. Draft 2 cut those and replaced them with engineered roster behaviour: armour that could not be staggered, hit-and-disengage ambushers, corpses that ruptured into smaller foes. Both were the same mistake with different paint. The ask was never for the maps to play differently.

So this draft asks nothing of the engine at all. No new spawner rules, no new combat behaviour, no per-map mechanic. The rosters fight the way everything in the Grove fights. The five maps are a place, a roster, a drop table and a bench — and the bench is where the work went.

Which is the right place for it, because the bench is where the repetition actually is. The generator in grove-maps.js says so in its own comment: “NO NEW KIND OF OBJECT is ever introduced after map one… from map two onward the bench is twelve tool ladders and a food stack, for eleven maps. A player at map nine is crafting the same twelve objects they crafted on day one in a new vocabulary.” Maps 8–12's 80 recipes exist today and not one of them was designed: toolRecipesForMap takes the first material of each of three roles, and foodRecipesForMap presses whatever is left over into the same four heals. That is the vanilla repeat, and it is mechanical.

The fix is not a new system. It is care. Luke: “Just be thoughtful when making each maps craftables.” So all 85 are authored below — each tool's three materials chosen rather than taken in order, each one named, each one saying what that place makes it into. A pickaxe of rib and long bone is not a pickaxe of floatstone and skywood, and the only thing that makes that true is somebody deciding it.

Where a thing genuinely should repeat, it repeats, and I have said so rather than inventing difference for its own sake.

The five

  • 08The Clockwork Deep — something built thisclockwork
  • 09The Frostmere — black water under white iceice
  • 10The Hollow Colossus — inside a dead titanflesh
  • 11The Cloudshelf — stone that forgot to fallcloud
  • 12The Starfall Rim — past the last airstar

Two names survive from the existing table — The Frostmere and The Starfall Rim, which were already right. Thunder Steppe, Glass Peaks and Rotgarden are retired: a steppe is grassland and a rotgarden is a swamp, and maps 1, 3 and 4 have covered green and wet thoroughly. See the note on what that costs in redrawn tool sprites.

What this had to work around

The unlock ladder is finished and stays finished. Towns 1–7 introduce every system the game has and that flow is not touched here. Draft 2 proposed five new town features — a foundry, a hearth, an infirmary, an aerie, an observatory — and all five are cut. Luke: “I want to leave the unlock flow as it is between worlds 1 and 7, and then don't worry about worlds 8 through 12's unlocks for features. We can deal with that over time.” Towns 8–12 are a store, a bench and the way down. Features arrive when there is a feature, not because a town needs one.

The map generator is honest about its shape. Each map is exactly 12 parts across 8 roles (edge haft bind hide ember glow stone charm), 12 creatures across 6 archetypes (swarm striker brute caster stalker grazer), 12 zones each with one boss, and one Mark. Nothing below changes that shape. What changes is that the recipes stop being derived from it.

One new stat, and it is a crafting stat. Four of the five Marks grant stats the game already consumes. The fifth, salvage on map 8, raises a rate that already exists in creatureRareLoot — a kill dropping a part from one map deeper. It survives the cut because it lands on the materials side, which is the side this draft is about.

⚠️ These maps are further along than “nothing is built” suggests. All five are already declared in grove-maps.js with parts, creatures, zones and bosses, 80 recipes are generated off them, and 60 tool sprites for tiers VIII–XII already exist on disk. What does not exist is any of it on screen: MAPS_BUILT = 7, and no zone, creature, boss or part art has been drawn past map 7.

⚠️ And renaming three of them has a bill. Maps 8, 10 and 11 are currently The Thunder Steppe, The Glass Peaks and The Rotgarden. Draft 2 retired the first and third as duplicates of ground maps 1, 3 and 4 already cover, and this draft keeps that call — but the 36 tool sprites for those three tiers were drawn from those maps' materials (tool-pick-t8 is a pickaxe of horn edge, lance shaft and bison hide) and would have to be redrawn. They have never been seen by a player, so this is not repainting shipped art; it is $1.52 and it belongs in the bill below.

08

The Clockwork Deep

“Something built this, and it was never told to stop.”

What map 8 is A stopped machine the size of a county, still running in places. Brutes and swarms with two stalkers under the floor and no grazers at all — nothing grazes in a machine — fought exactly the way everything in the Grove is fought. The difference is in the parts bin: every material here is manufactured rather than grown, which has not been true of any map before it, and the twelve tools made out of it are the first that look made rather than found.

The Mark

The Geared Mark — salvage, +1.6%/star.
NEW STAT. A kill has a chance to also drop a part from one map deeper. Consumer: the rare row in grove-roll.js, which already does exactly this for creatureRareLoot — this raises its rate rather than adding a mechanism.

Rare spawn

The First Light — The oldest thing in the game. Same mechanism as the stag. A rare roll, its own art, its own drop. Draft 2 gave each of the five a behaviour — compressing telegraphs, fleeing, healing the room. All cut: that is engine work, and five rares that are simply five different rares is the whole win here.

The Frostmere

“Black water under white ice, and the ice is the floor.”

What map 9 is Black water under white ice, and the ice is the floor. Stalker-heavy, because the threat is what is under you, and no swarms at all — nothing survives out here in numbers. Its materials are the only ones in the Grove that change in the hand: sinew that grips as it thaws, splinters that stay sharp for about a minute, a stone that holds a fire overnight. Four of its twelve tools get better the longer you work.

The Mark

The Hoar Mark — armour, +3/star.
EXISTING STAT, kept from the current table. A place trying to kill you slowly should give armour, and it is the one combat stat no deep-map Mark currently grants.

Rare spawn

The Mere-Lamp — a light under the ice that moves away as you approach and has to be cornered against a shelf edge. The first rare you catch by geometry rather than damage.

The Hollow Colossus

“It died standing, and the inside never got the message.”

What map 10 is Inside a dead titan that never got the message. Caster-heavy, because a body's defences are chemical — things here spray, bloom and infect rather than charge. Every material is anatomy, and that makes this the strangest bench in the game: a spade that pulls itself back out of the ground as the tendon dries, a lamp that burns a week on a thimble of bile, and the only draught in the Grove made out of something still beating.

The Mark

The Hollow Mark — life_steal, +2.2%/star.
EXISTING STAT, inherited from the retired Rotgarden. Taking life back inside a thing that is still trying to live is the single most on-theme stat in the table, and lifesteal is currently only granted by one map-one wanderer card.

Rare spawn

The Second Heart — heals every creature near it and never attacks. The first rare that is a priority target rather than a fight: ignore it and the room becomes unwinnable.

Decided and shipped after draft 2

Three calls landed in the hours after draft 2 was written, so none of them are in it. They are not proposals; they are in the game.

  1. Bag slot limits and the bank are gone (d5581415). Luke: “I think we get rid of the idea of a bank. And get rid of bag slot limits. And get rid of bank and backpack expansions. Its just a lame mechanic that is a meaningless tax on the player.” Sixteen distinct slots, the overflow map, the FULL badge, the “n slots left” warning, the second grid and eight expansion recipes: all deleted. Everything found is now both seen and stored. This matters to maps 8–12 directly — every argument the old recipes made about a sixteen-slot bag (“a part that drops and does nothing is litter in a sixteen-slot bag”) is now an argument about a bench instead, which is a better reason to author the recipes rather than generate them.
  2. Move speed is retired (d5581415). Off The Straw Man, the boss card rotation and the feet gear roll. Luke: “I also dont like move speed, seems like another meaningless factor we should not use.” It is the right call in a kill-rate game, and it is why no tool, charm or Mark below touches traversal.
  3. Statues shipped (64bfe4ee) — the answer to volume. Emblem-rare drops into five flat stat bars, the first crystal and the first prismatic each permanently doubling the line, no “turn in all”. It is the only thing in the Grove where the hundredth is worth as much as the first, and it is the reason maps 8–12 do not need a collection system of their own.

⚠️ And one doc is now wrong, not merely stale. docs/node-map-design.md §6 is still titled “Inventory and the bank” and still says “Backpack: 16 unique slots”, “Bank: up to 64 slots, located in town”, lists backpack I–V and vault I–VI, and at line 306 says “Food takes a BAG SLOT like a part does, which is the whole tension of it.” That describes a game that no longer exists. Corrected in the same pass as this draft.

What needs your call

  1. Read the craftables, not the maps. That is the whole draft. 60 tools, 20 foods and 5 charms are authored above and the test is simple: does map 10's bench read like a different place from map 11's, or does it read like the same twelve objects in new words? If any map fails that, it fails on the page and costs nothing to fix. Nothing else here is a question about variety any more.
  2. Hand-authored recipes mean the generators stop being the source. toolRecipesForMap and foodRecipesForMap currently build 80 recipes for these five maps from role order. Authoring them means those functions keep serving maps 2–7 and maps 8–12 carry explicit rows instead. That is more lines and less machinery, but it does mean check-grove.mjs has to check two shapes rather than one.
  3. One new stat. salvage (map 8) raises a rate that already exists in creatureRareLoot — a kill dropping a part from one map deeper. It is the only new code in the draft and it feeds crafting, which is why it survived. Say the word and it reuses an existing stat instead.
  4. Per-map rare spawns. One rare exists in the whole game today and all seven maps share it, so every hunt quest points at the same stag. Five named rares is five creatures of art and a GROVE_RARE that becomes a per-map lookup — no behaviour, no engine work. Cheapest real win on the list.
  5. Renaming maps 8, 10 and 11 costs 36 redrawn tool sprites ($1.52). They have never been seen by a player, so this is not repainting shipped art. The alternative is keeping Thunder Steppe, Glass Peaks and Rotgarden, which means maps 1, 3 and 4 have company they do not need.
  6. The statue numbers. Unrelated to these maps and still the open one: a statue of a given type every ~2,270 kills, level 10 at ~273,000. That wants your eye against the kill curve, not mine.
